# CV authorship data collection protocol

**Research plan, not permission to collect real CVs or evidence that authorship can be inferred.** The included examples and their human/AI/mixed/unknown labels are fictional software-test scenarios, not verified authorship or model performance. MAGE, HC3 and TXD-22 remain blocked. Do not use this workflow for applicant decisions or connect it to the public app or local inference model.

## Governance and consent before collection

Prefer invented CV-style tasks about fictional accomplishments over actual CVs. Before any participant collection, obtain independent ethics/legal and privacy review, define a research purpose, data controller, lawful basis, access controls, retention deadline, withdrawal process, incident response and documented permission for collection, processing, storage, transformation, research evaluation and any model training. Permission for one use is not permission for another, third-party sharing or publication. Avoid coercion and separate participation entirely from employment decisions. Explain limits on withdrawal after deletion, aggregation or model fitting. Maintain a separate access-controlled consent register and identity-to-random-ID mapping outside this repository, not in the manifest.

Require explicit, informed and freely given consent from every relevant contributor, including for source text and derivative AI-assisted variants. Validate consent and usage rights independently before changing metadata to `documented` / `approved`. Record pseudonymous references only. The command checks these **attestations**, not the actual consent documents, legal rights, identities, review quality or correctness; it cannot identify misrepresented fictional inputs. Unknown provenance is not a substitute for consent. Do not process a document when consent is missing, withdrawn, expired, or does not cover the intended processing; quarantine it outside the workflow pending review.

## Labels describe documented origin, not writing style

- **Human:** documented human creation, permitted research use and independent review of available drafts, workflow and author attestation; document any tool restrictions and uncertainty. Attestation alone is not proof. Never infer human authorship from style.
- **AI:** record the generator/model and version when available, date, approved prompt-family ID and generation-settings reference, and retain authorized generation evidence separately. Model provider terms and output rights require review. Never send participant data to a model without specific authorization. This tool does not call models or external services.
- **Mixed:** document the human source, any AI-assisted editing instructions, relevant human editing steps and transformation history. Link each variant to a consented human parent. Preserve revision evidence and applicable permissions; a model-produced paraphrase of human source is not automatically purely AI-authored. The current schema records controlled editing codes and a settings reference, not actual prompt contents. Keep detailed instructions and evidence in the secure register, without personal identifiers in metadata.
- **Unknown:** use when creation history is incomplete or contested; document the reason in the restricted evidence register and do not silently assign human or AI. Consent and review are still required. Separately report these examples; do not quietly include them in binary ground truth.

Author-provenance labels are claims about a documented creation process, not aesthetic judgments. Human, AI and edited writing overlap. Short formulaic CV sections, accessibility needs, language background, tools and editing can create false associations. No score or dataset alone proves authorship, validates a detector for CVs or justifies a hiring outcome.

## Minimization, withdrawal and retention

Do not request names, email, telephone, addresses, account identifiers or unnecessary employer/school/date details. Inspect all text and metadata in a restricted environment; automated redaction is imperfect and **does not replace consent or manual review**. Use randomly generated identifiers that do not encode personal information. Keep real source files, metadata, registers, backups and prepared outputs outside the repository in encrypted, access-controlled storage. `.gitignore` is only an accident-prevention aid, not access control. Do not publish raw CVs, personal details, linkable hashes, logs or per-person outputs without a separate lawful basis and specific authorization; pseudonymous data and hashes can be reidentified.

Provide a clear withdrawal contact and procedure in the consent materials. On receipt, verify authority in the separate consent register, stop processing, identify the original and linked derivatives via the register and parent/group links, and remove them from source, prepared manifests, working copies and backups according to the approved deletion and backup-expiry policy. Review affected splits, evaluations and any derived model: halt use and retrain or delete as required by the approved protocol. Notify recipients where feasible, document completion without storing the withdrawn text, and explain any previously disclosed limits on withdrawal. Set and enforce a concrete approved retention date; verify deletion of accessible copies and encryption keys where appropriate. Filesystem deletion alone does not guarantee physical erasure. Do not assume `Remove-Item` covers backups or remote copies.

## Local manifest and preparation

Input is an explicit metadata JSON array and an explicit local directory containing UTF-8 text files named `<record_id>.txt`. Only listed files are opened; there is no crawling, upload integration, external service or automatic generation. The [fictional metadata example](../backend/ml/fixtures/cv_collection/metadata.json) and accompanying `.txt` files demonstrate the exact required fields. Record IDs must be opaque `r-` plus eight lowercase hexadecimal digits; group and consent references use `g-` / `c-` plus eight hex digits. They are not names or contact details. Avoid collisions by assigning random IDs under study governance; this tiny format does not guarantee uniqueness across studies.

Each row contains `record_id`, `provenance_group`, `related_groups`, `label`, `source_status`, `consent_status`, `consent_ref`, `collection_method`, `document_type`, `language`, `prompt_family`, `model_id`, `model_version`, `generation_settings_ref`, `human_editing_history`, `parent_record_id`, `privacy_review` and `rights_review`. Unknown metadata keys are rejected. `rights_review` and `privacy_review` must be `approved`; `source_status` is `fictional` or `consented-local` with respectively `fictional` or `documented` consent. The latter is an operator attestation, not a consent verification mechanism. The AI and mixed labels require a model ID, prompt-family group ID and settings reference; version may be null if genuinely unavailable. Mixed rows require a human parent in the same input batch, matching consent reference and source status, and a nonempty list of controlled editing codes. Collection method must match the label. IDs, model names, language and references must be non-identifying controlled codes: validation cannot reliably recognize all personal data inside codes.

Preparation extends the Day 14 schema: it computes a normalized text hash, sets `original_record_id`, `source_domain`, and `split`, and links prompt families and parent provenance groups through `related_groups`. `source_url` is a fixed example.org placeholder, not a claim of actual origin or a rights source; keep actual source evidence in the secure register. Fictional rows retain `unverified` license status; for consented-local rows `approved` only reflects the supplied attestation and is not legal clearance. All inputs and generated text for participant records must remain outside the repository; only the bundled fictional fixture directory and its metadata are accepted as repository-local inputs. The CLI requires an **existing absolute output directory outside any Git working tree**, with a regular `.gitignore` containing only `*`; this marker is a precaution, not protection if files are later moved or force-added. It creates `prepared.json` exclusively and will not overwrite an existing file. The output **contains full input texts**, metadata and hashes: protect and delete it as sensitive data. Do not use a shared or adversarial local filesystem; path checks and ignore rules are not a defense against malicious local processes, race conditions, junctions on all platforms, backups or filesystem snapshots.

Before reading text the command checks all metadata. Then it detects normalized exact duplicates (rejects them), reports possible near-duplicate index pairs, and connects known provenance groups, prompt families, parent-child relationships and near duplicates for deterministic train/validation/test planning using the Day 14 audit. At least three independent components are required; all three splits must be nonempty. Review every near pair, group assignment, split distribution, provenance and consent offline before considering use. Heuristics can miss paraphrases, hidden relationships, personal details and overlaps, or overmerge boilerplate; passing does not prove independence. The quadratic comparisons are for small batches only. Planning does not guarantee class balance, source representativeness or permission to train. Group and split stability may change when data or seed changes; freeze independently reviewed test sets before experiments.

For fictional-only local testing on Windows PowerShell, from `backend`:

```powershell
$out = Join-Path $env:TEMP 'cvdetect-fictional-collection'
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Path $out
Set-Content -Path (Join-Path $out '.gitignore') -Value '*' -Encoding ascii
python -m ml.cv_collection --metadata ml/fixtures/cv_collection/metadata.json --input-dir ml/fixtures/cv_collection --output-dir $out
```

Choose a new empty directory if `prepared.json` exists; the command will not replace it. Review the fictional `prepared.json` locally, including `near_duplicate_pairs`, labels and splits. Delete only the test output and marker after review: `Remove-Item (Join-Path $out 'prepared.json'), (Join-Path $out '.gitignore')` and then `Remove-Item $out` if the directory is empty. For real research follow the approved retention and withdrawal process instead. No real collection or training is approved by this document.
